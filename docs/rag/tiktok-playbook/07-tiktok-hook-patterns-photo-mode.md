## How TikTok Photo Mode Processes Engagement Signals

Unlike Instagram carousels (which measure swipe-through velocity), TikTok Photo Mode uses a **completion rate** model: if someone swipes through all slides, TikTok counts it as a "completed view" — equivalent to a video that was watched 100%. This is algorithmically identical to a video's watch-time signal, which is TikTok's primary distribution lever. Implication: every slide must earn the next swipe, not just the first.

---

## Rules

1. **First slide must hook in 1.5 seconds flat.** TikTok's feed autoplays photo mode with a brief first-slide preview. The hook window is now approximately 1.5 seconds (down from 3 seconds). For static text: the hook phrase must be readable in 1.5 seconds, meaning a maximum of 6–8 words in large type on slide 1. Use the same urgency as a video hook but in text form.

2. **First slide = thumbnail AND hook.** On TikTok, Photo Mode's first slide appears in the FYP before the user taps to view. The slide must: (a) stop the scroll visually, and (b) communicate a clear value promise that makes swiping feel worth the effort. For B2B tech: bold claim text in the largest font on the slide, ideally overlaying a contrasting visual background. Example: "We broke production with this one LLM setting" in large white text over a dark background.

3. **Compress the hook formula for TikTok's faster consumption context:**
   - Instagram carousel hook: can be a complete sentence with supporting clause
   - TikTok Photo Mode hook: must be a compressed fragment or 1-sentence bomb
   - "Stop using this RAG pattern" — works on TikTok
   - "In this carousel I'm going to walk you through the specific RAG pattern that teams make mistakes with" — does not work on TikTok's first slide

4. **Mirror the hook on slide 1 AND in the caption's first line.** TikTok indexes both. The first line of the caption appears below the slide when the user is viewing the carousel. Consistent hook language between slide 1 and caption first line reinforces the message and improves in-app search ranking.

5. **Use "curiosity cliffhanger" between slides.** On TikTok, each slide's last sentence should create a reason to see the next slide: "Mistake #1 cost us 3 hours of downtime — slide 2 is worse" or "Slide 4 is the one most teams miss." This explicit slide-to-slide curiosity tension is more important on TikTok than on Instagram because TikTok users have a shorter patience window before scrolling to the next post.

## Photo Mode vs. Video Hook Difference

Photo Mode does not have a verbal hook (no voiceover unless operator records one). This means the text on slide 1 does ALL the work that a video's first spoken line would do. The hook must be self-contained, visually prominent, and immediately scannable — there is no ambient audio + visuals combination to slow the viewer down.

---

## Anti-Patterns

- First slide that is a brand logo or author headshot with no text — fails the 1.5-second hook window
- First slide text in small font — unreadable in autoplay preview
- No slide-to-slide curiosity tension — leads to drop-off after slide 2–3, hurts completion rate
- Hook that requires context to understand — TikTok FYP serves to non-followers; assume zero prior knowledge
- Telling viewers to "check the comments" or "link in bio" on slide 1 — wastes the hook on a navigation prompt
